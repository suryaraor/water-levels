# Kaleshwaram Stage 1 — Pump House Research (Medigadda → Annaram → Sundilla → Yellampally)

## Summary
- Source: Godavari River at Medigadda / Lakshmi Barrage (bed level ~89 m, pond/FRL ~100 m)
- Destination: Yellampally Reservoir / Sripada Yellampally Project (FRL ~148 m)
- Total lift across the stage: ~48–56 m (sources disagree on the exact per-hop breakdown; see conflicts section). Telangana's own chain data (per this project's `chains.json`) uses 8 + 20 + 10 + 18 = 56 m; barrage FRL deltas from Wikipedia/Grokipedia (100 → ~119–120 → 130 → 148) imply roughly 19–20 + 10–11 + 18 m.
- Structural reality check: **"Link‑1" of KLIS officially comprises only three pump houses** — Lakshmi (at Medigadda/Kannepalli), Saraswathi (at Annaram/Siripuram or Gunjapadaga — sources differ), and Parvathi (at Sundilla/Golivada). The Parvathi pump house is the one that discharges into the Yellampally reservoir; no separate "Yellampally pump house" performing an inbound lift is documented in any source found. See Data Conflicts below.
- Status overview: All three Link‑1 barrages (Medigadda, Annaram, Sundilla) have been under a National Dam Safety Authority (NDSA) safety ban on water storage since the October 2023 Medigadda pier subsidence. As of July 2026 news coverage, rehabilitation designs/scientific testing are in final stages, with officials targeting full rehabilitation "by next summer" (i.e., ~2027) and earlier statements that pumps "cannot be started before 2027." Some limited pumping activity was reported in August 2025 news items, but the barrages remain officially barred from full water storage pending NDSA/CWC clearance.

## Pump Houses

### Lakshmi Pump House (a.k.a. "Medigadda pump house", "Kannepalli/Kannepally lift station")
- Location: Kannepalli (Kannepally) village, near Medigadda village, Mahadevpur Mandal, Jayashankar Bhupalpally district
- Associated barrage/lift step: Godavari River at Medigadda (Lakshmi Barrage, FRL 100 m) → Annaram (Saraswathi Barrage)
- Number of pumping units: **17** (widely cited final figure — Telangana Today, The Hans India). Note: MEIL's own June 2019 press release described only 11 units in the "first phase" (440 MW), with 6 more units under dry/wet run testing at the time; a MEIL project page states "12 machines" were installed. 17 units appears to be the completed/final count reported in later coverage.
- Unit capacity (MW): 40 MW (per Hans India, Telangana Today); one MEIL press release also cites 40 MW/unit for the phase‑1 count. A separate, less-corroborated figure of 139 MW/unit and "2,376 tonnes per motor" appears in one Telangana Today piece, but this figure matches the *Gayatri* pump house's 139 MW motors, so it is likely a conflation/error in that article.
- Total installed capacity (MW): **680 MW** (17 × 40 MW), per multiple 2020s sources. Grokipedia's Link‑1 breakdown independently states "Lakshmi Pump House: 17 pumps, 40 MW each, 680 MW total," corroborating this.
- Lift (m): Reported inconsistently — one MEIL/PR Newswire release says pumps sit "91 m above the Godavari River" (ambiguous whether that's lift or platform elevation); Grokipedia's Link‑1 breakdown states lift = 49 m; this project's own `chains.json` uses 8 m for Medigadda's own lift-from-Godavari step (with the 20 m step attributed to Annaram). None of these fully reconcile; treat lift height as **not reliably established** across sources (see conflicts).
- Discharge capacity: 60 cusecs/unit cited in one PR Newswire piece → ~660–1,020 cusecs total depending on unit count used; separately, capacity to lift "3 TMC of water every day" (2 TMC/day in earlier DPR figures) is repeatedly cited for the full Medigadda stage.
- Pump/motor type: Vertical, large custom motor-pump sets ("Baahubali motors"); one source specifies pumps sourced from Finland and Austria manufacturers; electromechanical works by BHEL, Andritz, WILO/Jyoti (various suppliers named across sources, not fully consistent).
- Commissioned / status: Inaugurated 21 June 2019 by then-CM K. Chandrashekar Rao ("world's biggest pumphouse" per MEIL/PR Newswire). Operational status since Oct 2023: Pillar No. 20 in Block‑7 sank ~1.256 m by 24 Oct 2023 following piping/foundation failure; NDSA's 2025 post-monsoon review classified Medigadda in "Category I" (highest risk) and recommended full removal/reconstruction of Block‑7. As of mid‑2026, barrage storage remains banned pending repairs; some limited "ready to resume" pumping reported by Telangana Today, but not full operation.
- Notes: Pump house is popularly called the "world's biggest pumphouse" at the time of 2019 inauguration (MEIL/PR Newswire claim). Twenty-seven motors were reported submerged during a July 2022 flood event (Deccan Chronicle / Hans India), an early sign of the site's flood/foundation vulnerability later implicated in the 2023 failure.

### Saraswathi Pump House (a.k.a. "Annaram (Medaram) pump house")
- Location: Annaram village, Mahadevpur Mandal, Jayashankar Bhupalpally district (Saraswati Barrage). The associated pump house's exact hamlet is disputed across sources — variously named **Siripuram** and **Gunjapadaga**; "Medaram" (used in this project's chains.json) also appears associated with the general area/pump-house naming discussion but was not confirmed as the pump house's precise village in any single authoritative source.
- Associated barrage/lift step: Medigadda (100 m) → Annaram (Saraswathi Barrage, FRL ~119–120 m; capacity cited as both 10.87 TMC and 11.9 TMC across sources)
- Number of pumping units: Conflicting — **12 units** (Hans India, Telangana Today, both independently) vs **8 units** (Grokipedia Link‑1 breakdown; also a MEIL/PR Newswire 2019 figure describing early-phase installation).
- Unit capacity (MW): 40 MW (consistent across all sources found).
- Total installed capacity (MW): **480 MW** if 12 units (Hans India/Telangana Today basis) or 320 MW if 8 units (Grokipedia basis). The combined "43 machines / 1,720 MW across Link‑1" figure (cited independently by both Hans India and Telangana Today) is only consistent with the 17+12+14=43 breakdown, which supports 12 units as the more likely correct total for Annaram.
- Lift (m): Grokipedia cites 34 m for the Saraswathi pump house lift; not independently corroborated by another numeric source found. This project's chains.json models the Medigadda→Annaram hop as 20 m.
- Discharge capacity: One source cites "11,724 cusecs" lifted at Saraswathi; not corroborated elsewhere and units/context (instantaneous discharge vs. cumulative) unclear.
- Pump/motor type: Not specifically detailed beyond "40 MW vertical motor-pump sets," consistent with Link‑1 pump house design generally.
- Commissioned / status: Commissioned alongside the rest of Link‑1 around 2019 (barrage construction 2 May 2016 – 21 June 2019, per Wikipedia's Lakshmi Barrage page timeline, generally cited for the whole Link‑1 group). Status since 2023: NDSA's April 2024 assessment found "seepage, piping, and dislodgment of downstream wearing coats/CC blocks" at Annaram; barrage placed under the same no-storage restriction as Medigadda and Sundilla pending repair; EoIs for rehabilitation design were sought with an October 2025 deadline.
- Notes: Along with Parvathi (Sundilla) and Gayatri (package‑8) pump houses, Saraswathi is cited as having "lifted 100 TMC of water" as an operational milestone (Hans India).
- Sources: https://www.thehansindia.com/telangana/kaleshwaram-lifted-100tmc-water-by-each-pump-house-677112 ; https://telanganatoday.com/kaleshwaram-to-be-back-in-action-soon-all-35-pumping-units-at-three-barrages-ready ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraswati_Barrage ; https://thesouthfirst.com/telangana/after-ndsa-red-flag-telangana-seeks-designs-for-medigadda-annaram-sundilla-repairs/ ; https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/telangana/klis-barrages-need-extensive-repair-ndsa-1875014

### Parvathi Pump House (a.k.a. "Sundilla pump house")
- Location: Golivada (pump house), near Kasipeta village, Manthani Mandal, Peddapalli district (Parvati/Sundilla Barrage)
- Associated barrage/lift step: Annaram (~120 m) → Sundilla (Parvathi Barrage, bed level 118 m, pond level 130 m, storage 8.83 TMC) → discharges onward into Yellampally Reservoir
- Number of pumping units: Conflicting — **14 units** (Hans India, Telangana Today) vs **9 units** (Grokipedia/MEIL 2019 early-phase figure).
- Unit capacity (MW): 40 MW (consistent across sources).
- Total installed capacity (MW): **560 MW** if 14 units (consistent with the 43-unit/1,720 MW Link‑1 total cited by two independent sources) or 360 MW if 9 units.
- Lift (m): Reported as **40 m** in one source ("these pumps lift water to a height of 40 m and discharge 656 cusecs of water into Yellampalli barrage"); Grokipedia also states 40 m for the Parvathi pump house lift — this is the one lift-height figure with two independent corroborating mentions. However it does not match this project's chains.json figure of 10 m for the Annaram→Sundilla hop, nor the ~18 m figure attributed to the Sundilla→Yellampally hop; it's unclear whether the "40 m" figure represents total dynamic head (including friction/delivery losses) rather than simple elevation difference.
- Discharge capacity: 656 cusecs discharged into Yellampalli barrage per one source; a separate figure of "10,440 cusecs" lifted at Parvathi also appears in another source, with no clear reconciliation between the two (likely different measurement points — pump-house discharge vs. combined channel flow).
- Pump/motor type: Not specifically detailed beyond "40 MW vertical motor-pump sets," consistent with Link‑1 design generally.
- Commissioned / status: Commissioned as part of Link‑1, ~2019 (barrage construction 2 May 2016 – 21 June 2019). Status since 2023: NDSA's April 2024 review found seepage/piping issues at Sundilla similar to Annaram; barrage under a no-storage restriction; "Complete Sundilla barrage restoration work on a war footing" reported by Hans India as an ongoing 2025-era priority; monitoring instrumentation (Encardio-Rite) has been installed for structural health monitoring.
- Notes: This is the final Link‑1 pump house — it delivers water that fills the Yellampally reservoir, meaning the "Yellampally pump house" listed separately in this project's chains.json may in fact be describing this same physical lift step rather than a distinct fourth facility (see Data Conflicts).
- Sources: https://www.thehansindia.com/telangana/kaleshwaram-lifted-100tmc-water-by-each-pump-house-677112 ; https://telanganatoday.com/kaleshwaram-to-be-back-in-action-soon-all-35-pumping-units-at-three-barrages-ready ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parvati_Barrage ; https://www.encardio.com/projects/kaleswaram-sundilla-barrage-india ; https://www.thehansindia.com/telangana/complete-sundilla-barrage-restoration-work-on-a-war-footing-uttam-kumar-883474

### "Yellampally pump house" — no distinct facility confirmed; likely name conflation
- Location: Sripada Yellampalli Project barrage/reservoir is at Yellampalli village, Ramagundam (rural) mandal, spanning Peddapalli and Mancherial districts (FRL/storage ~20 TMC after floodgates; height 26.3 m; length 1,180.7 m; 62 gates).
- Associated barrage/lift step: Sundilla (130 m) → Yellampally Reservoir (148 m)
- Number of pumping units: **Not found** as a distinct facility separate from the Parvathi (Sundilla) pump house described above, which is the component that actually discharges into Yellampally.
- Unit capacity (MW): Not applicable / not found for a distinct "Yellampally pump house."
- Total installed capacity (MW): Not found as a distinct facility. (Note: Wikipedia's Sripada Yellampalli Project page separately states the project itself — as a standalone, pre-Kaleshwaram irrigation scheme — requires "163 MW power and 469 million kWh annually" to run its own lift-canal system unrelated to Kaleshwaram Link‑1.)
- Lift (m): See Parvathi pump house entry — Sundilla → Yellampally is the terminal hop of Link‑1, most plausibly executed by the Parvathi pump house itself (40 m dynamic head, or ~18 m by elevation-difference reckoning per this project's chains.json).
- Discharge capacity: See Parvathi pump house entry (656 cusecs cited).
- Pump/motor type: Not applicable — no distinct facility found.
- Commissioned / status: Not applicable — no distinct facility found. Yellampalli/Sripada barrage itself is a pre-existing (older) irrigation project later integrated into Kaleshwaram as the Link‑1 terminus and Link‑2 origin.
- Notes: **Important distinction** — the pump house most people mean when discussing lifting *out of* Yellampally is the **Gayatri Pump House** (a.k.a. "Lakshmipur Underground Pumping Station," Package‑8), located near Lakshmipur village, Karimnagar district. It is described as the world's largest underground irrigation pump house, built ~178 m below reverse surface level, with **7 units of 139 MW each = 973 MW total**, discharging ~3,150 cusecs per machine and lifting ~2 TMC/day from Yellampally reservoir up to the Mid Manair Dam. This is **Stage 2 / Link‑2 of KLIS, not Stage 1** — it moves water away from Yellampally, not into it — so it should not be conflated with a "Yellampally pump house" performing the inbound Stage‑1 lift, even though project literature and this project's own chains.json (which separately lists "Ramadugu · Kannepalli · Gandhari pump houses" for the Yellampally→Mid Manair hop) associates the Gayatri/Ramadugu facility with the Yellampally area.

## Data conflicts / uncertainty
1. **Pump house naming/count for Stage 1**: Multiple authoritative sources (news coverage, MEIL/contractor materials) describe Link‑1 as having only **three** pump houses (Lakshmi/Medigadda, Saraswathi/Annaram, Parvathi/Sundilla) that together lift Godavari water from Medigadda into the Yellampally reservoir. No source found documents a fourth, distinct "Yellampally pump house" performing an inbound lift. The chains.json in this project's data may be conflating the Sundilla→Yellampally hop (done by the Parvathi pump house) with a nonexistent separate facility, or may be intentionally modeling it as a logical "step" rather than a physical pump house — worth clarifying with the app's data maintainers.
2. **Unit counts diverge by source and possibly by date**: Medigadda is reported as 11 units (June 2019, phase‑1/MEIL), 12 units (MEIL project page), or 17 units (later 2020s coverage — Hans India, Telangana Today). Annaram is reported as 8 or 12 units; Sundilla as 9 or 14 units. The **17+12+14 = 43 units, 1,720 MW** combination is independently corroborated by two separate outlets (The Hans India and Telangana Today) reporting the same aggregate figures, so it is treated as the best-supported total in this report, but the smaller figures (11/8/9, totaling ~28 units) likely reflect an earlier construction/commissioning snapshot (~2019) rather than an error.
3. **Lift height per pump house is poorly documented and inconsistent**: Figures found range from Grokipedia's 49 m (Lakshmi)/34 m (Saraswathi)/40 m (Parvathi) to a "91 m above Godavari River" figure for Medigadda in a MEIL press release, to this project's own chains.json figures of 8/20/10/18 m per hop. None of these fully reconcile with each other or with the barrage FRL deltas (100 → ~119–120 → 130 → 148 m ≈ 19–20 + 10–11 + 18 m). No authoritative single source (e.g., DPR or CAG report table) with a clean per-pump-house lift breakdown could be retrieved — the CAG 2024 audit report PDF (Chapter V, "Execution of Kaleshwaram Project") could not be parsed for tabular data in this research pass.
4. **Discharge/capacity figures**: Medigadda's total lifting capacity is cited as both "660 cusecs" and "2–3 TMC/day," and unit-level cusecs figures (60 cusecs/unit at Medigadda; 11,724 cusecs at Saraswathi; 656 vs. 10,440 cusecs at Parvathi) do not cleanly cross-multiply against the reported unit counts, suggesting different sources are measuring at different points (single unit vs. full barrage vs. cumulative channel flow) or reporting errors exist in secondary press coverage.
5. **Annaram barrage storage capacity**: cited as both 10.87 TMC (this project's chains.json / some press) and 11.9 TMC (Grokipedia) — a minor but unresolved discrepancy.
6. **Pump house village-level location for Annaram**: "Siripuram," "Gunjapadaga," and "Medaram" all appear across different sources as the pump house's precise hamlet; could not be conclusively resolved to one name.
7. **Current operational status** as of the August 2026 research date is itself contested: official ministerial statements (Uttam Kumar Reddy, Telangana irrigation minister) say pumps "cannot be started before 2027," while other 2025-era social media/news items describe pumps briefly "roaring back into action" for limited/emergency purposes (e.g., flood-season operation from a location called "Nandi Medaram"). Treat any claim of full Stage‑1 operational status as unconfirmed pending further/primary-source verification.
